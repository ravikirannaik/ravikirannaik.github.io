I work on education--occupation mismatch and the returns to human capital, the questions at the centre of your subproject.

\vspace{0.1in}

\textbf{Research fit.}
I study education--occupation mismatch --- its wage consequences, whether it works as a stepping stone or becomes a trap, and what role informality plays in distorting returns. In ``Educational Mismatch, Wage Returns, and Occupational Mobility: Evidence from the NLSY79'' (under submission), I use Double/Debiased Machine Learning and causal forests on 25 years of NLSY79 panel data and find that the stepping-stone effect is front-loaded, declining by over 70\% within four waves; a companion paper extends this to urban India using PLFS data. ``Analysis of Returns to Education-Occupation Mismatch in Presence of Informality'' (under review, \textit{Labour Economics}) shows how informality distorts matched-employment returns. Two further papers take up gender (``Gender Differences in Returns to Education Mismatch,'' forthcoming, Springer Nature) and caste (``Stratified Modernisation,'' under review, \textit{Journal of Development Economics}, with S.~Ravindra). The PhD thesis I completed at JNU in 2020, on intergenerational economic mobility, connects to the life-course perspective of Subproject P2.

An ICSSR-funded project on digital labour platforms --- a primary survey of 2,000+ workers across Ahmedabad and Pune --- extends the mismatch question to platform work: does skill specialisation generate earning differentials, or does algorithmic management erode them? A related paper draws on three rounds of the PLFS ($N = 1{,}268{,}400$) to trace how AI adoption is reshaping entry-level employment.

\vspace{0.1in}

\textbf{Methods and data.}
I work with large-scale survey data (PLFS, IHDS, NLSY79, primary surveys) and use panel data estimation, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables, and semiparametric methods (DML, causal forests). Stata is my primary software; I also use Python. I co-directed field data collection for the ICSSR platform workers study and have contributed to multi-year survey projects at IRMA and IIHS.

\vspace{0.1in}

I can teach Master's-level labour economics and applied econometrics in English from the start.
